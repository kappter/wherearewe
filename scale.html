How far apart

Shrink one object to a familiar size—a basketball, baseball, or golf ball. The tool scales its partner and the distance between them by the same ratio. Then try hopping from our neighborhood to the galaxy next door.

A regulation-size basketball represents Earth. The Moon keeps the same size ratio and the real average Earth–Moon separation.

Constellation explorer

✳
How the scale works: stand-in diameter ÷ real diameter of object A = scale factor. Multiply object B’s real diameter and the real separation by that factor. Distances are center-to-center; planets use average or mean distances. Galaxy “center” comparisons treat the centers as points. Galaxy and nebula distances and diameters are rounded illustrative estimates; measured extents vary by source and by the boundary used. Constellation sketches show apparent patterns, not physical star groupings. Pea (8 mm) and sand (0.5 mm) sizes are representative. “Hydrogen nucleus” means one proton; 1.68 fm is an illustrative diameter, twice the proton’s rms charge radius, not a hard edge.
